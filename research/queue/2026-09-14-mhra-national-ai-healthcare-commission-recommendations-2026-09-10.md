# MHRA-commissioned National Commission on AI in Healthcare publishes UK regulatory recommendations (10 Sep 2026)

## Summary
The National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare — an independent body the MHRA established in September 2025, led by NHS clinicians with international contributors from the US and Singapore — published its recommendations for a future UK AI-in-healthcare regulatory framework on 10 September 2026. It drew on evidence from over 12,000 patients, clinicians, healthcare leaders, industry and technology developers over roughly a year. Its four headline recommendations: (1) *staged authorisations* for new AI models — explicitly compared to L-plates for learner drivers, with supervised, tightly guardrailed early deployment before broader clearance; (2) *continuous, real-world monitoring* of AI-enabled medical devices for their entire working life, not just at initial approval, reflecting that model behaviour drifts post-deployment; (3) a *public, searchable database* of safety information and adverse incidents for specific AI-enabled devices; (4) *enhanced MHRA enforcement powers* to act decisively when AI systems fall short of expected standards. Public engagement findings cited alongside the report found broad support for AI in healthcare conditioned on strong safety standards, meaningful human oversight, and transparency about when/how AI is used in someone's care.

## Why flagged (tie to CardiTrack specifically)
This is a recommendations report to the MHRA, not law or guidance — it changes nothing today. But it is the clearest UK signal yet of the *shape* the AIaMD framework (expected later in 2026, per the MHRA Software Group / AI Airlock findings) will take, and every one of its four asks bears directly on CardiTrack's architecture:
- **Staged authorisation ('L-plates')**: if adopted, a new MedGemma model version or a new severity-routing behaviour could require a supervised rollout period under regulatory guardrails before full release — this would directly affect how we manage model/prompt changes in the AI ingestion pipeline (`docs/llm_design.md`), not just clinical claims.
- **Continuous real-world monitoring**: CardiTrack already logs and reviews narration output, but "continuous monitoring for the working life of the device" as a *regulatory* (not just internal QA) obligation would be new territory, and blurs today's assumption that narration-only output keeps us off the SaMD hook — monitoring obligations are traditionally a SaMD/post-market surveillance feature.
- **Public safety/incident database**: would require us to have an incident-reporting and disclosure process built and ready, not just internal.
- **Enforcement powers**: raises the cost of getting the SaMD/non-SaMD classification wrong.
None of this is actionable today, but it should inform how conservatively we scope the MedGemma narration boundary (narrate-not-diagnose) going forward, since the direction of travel is toward *more* oversight of AI clinical-adjacent software, not less — a UK-specific counterpoint to FDA's parallel wellness/CDS deregulation.
