# Draft Medical Devices (Amendment) Regulations 2026: the UK pre-market regime, expected to be laid Q4 2026 and to apply to GB market access around mid-2027 — includes a rule that claims must match the stated intended purpose

**Severity:** FYI
**Category:** regulation
**Date recorded:** 2026-09-26

## Summary

The draft **Medical Devices (Amendment) Regulations 2026**, amending the UK Medical Devices
Regulations 2002, were published for WTO comment on 2026-05-08 with an MHRA news item inviting views
and a stakeholder impact survey that closed 2026-06-19. Search snippets put the laying in Parliament
at Q4 2026 ("December 2026"), the GB-market-access provisions in force around June 2027, and the
international-reliance provisions in Q2/Q3 2028.

**Content as reported:** essential requirements aligned to international practice; IVD classification
aligned to IMDRF; technical-documentation retention; custom-made device traceability; and — the line
that matters here — "requiring manufacturers to align claims about their medical devices with their
statement of intended purpose".

**Where CardiTrack stands.** Not on the digest record and not cited in any compliance document. No
obligation attaches to a product that is not a device, and OI-2 (the device-classification decision)
is still open. Two things to log regardless: (1) the timetable is the UKCA-side deadline OI-2 should be
planned against if the answer is "device" — a mid-2027 application date sits inside the R3/R4 waves;
(2) the claims-must-match-intended-purpose rule is the statutory form of the MHRA Ambient Voice
precedent recorded 2026-09-04. The marketing copy on carditrack.com and the MedGemma tone block
("Never diagnose or invent a condition") are the claims; the DPIA §3 wording ("early-warning system
based on trends and patterns, not a diagnostic tool") is the anchor they must not exceed.

**Unverified.** The draft text was not read (gov.uk proxy-blocked). Whether it changes the software
qualification test or the UK equivalent of EU MDR Rule 11 — the current UK rules lean on the same
logic — is the open question; the snippets are silent on it.
