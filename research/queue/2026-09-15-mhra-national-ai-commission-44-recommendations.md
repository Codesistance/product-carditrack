# MHRA's National Commission publishes 44 recommendations for AI-in-healthcare regulation

**Category:** regulation · **Severity:** FYI (no compliance deadline yet — but shapes the boundary this routine watches)

## Summary

Published 2026-09-14, following consultation with 12,000+ clinicians and
patients. Headline proposal: **"staged authorisations"** for AI models —
an "L-plate"-style graduated risk-tier system — plus lifecycle monitoring,
mandatory AI-safety-incident tracking, and a patient right to be told when AI
is involved in their care. This is the Commission's recommendation set, not
yet MHRA's own framework or a rule with a compliance date; MHRA's formal
response and any legislative vehicle (the Health Bill reform powers already
tracked, 2026-09-09) come next.

This directly follows on from the MHRA AI Airlock Phase 2 completion flagged
2026-09-04 ("dedicated AI-in-medical-devices framework expected later in
2026") — this is that framework's first concrete shape.

## Why flagged

CardiTrack's standing regulatory question is whether its feature set sits on
the SaMD side of the line. A tiered/staged model — rather than a binary
SaMD/non-SaMD cut — could change how a severity-narration feature like
MedGemma's output gets classified: a low-tier AI function might get a
lighter-touch pathway, or the tiering could formalise lifecycle/incident-
reporting duties for any AI that touches clinical judgment, which
CardiTrack's severity narration arguably does today.

## Next question

When MHRA formally responds to the Commission, does the staged-authorisation
tiering place "human-reviewed narrative output, no automated action" tools
(CardiTrack's current design, consistent with the MHRA Ambient Voice
Technology precedent already on file) in a lightweight tier, or does it fold
all AI health-severity narration into a mandatory registration/monitoring
tier regardless of automation level?
