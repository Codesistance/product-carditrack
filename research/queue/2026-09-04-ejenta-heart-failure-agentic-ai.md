# Ejenta — NASA-derived agentic AI, heart-failure remote monitoring, Best in Show at HIMSS26

**Severity:** HIGH
**Category:** competition

## Summary

Ejenta's agentic-AI platform ingests biosensor/wearable data, runs autonomous
monitoring agents, and generates predictive alerts for heart-failure patients. Its
Point32Health (Harvard Pilgrim/Tufts) deployment won Best in Show for total-cost-of-care
reduction at HIMSS26 (2026-03).

## Sources

- https://swaay.health/2026/03/26/best-of-show-himss26/ (award coverage)
- https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/point32health-to-pilot-ejenta-remote-monitoring-system-to-help-improve-health-and-wellness-of-congestive-heart-failure-patients-301572973.html (background on the deployment)

## Why flagged

The closest technical analog found to CardiTrack's core loop: continuous wearable/
biosensor data → AI agent → predictive cardiac alert. The key difference — and
CardiTrack's actual moat — is audience: Ejenta routes alerts to payer/clinical care
teams, not family caregivers, and produces care-team-facing output rather than a
plain-language family digest narrative. Worth tracking closely since the technical
pattern is identical even though the go-to-market differs.

## Question to answer next

Watch for any signal Ejenta (or a similar payer/clinical-facing platform) moves toward a
direct-to-family/caregiver product surface — that would be the point this becomes a
direct competitive threat rather than an adjacent one.
