# FDA discussion paper on Generative-AI-Enabled Medical Devices — comment period open to Oct 19

**Category:** regulation · **Severity:** FYI (comment period, not a compliance deadline — but live and worth acting on)

## Summary

FDA's Digital Health Center of Excellence (CDRH) released "Considerations for
the Regulation of Generative AI-Enabled Medical Devices" on 2026-08-18
(docket FDA-2026-N-7874), the first FDA framework document to treat
generative-model output — as opposed to discriminative/predictive AI — as its
own risk category. It proposes a two-axis risk-assessment framework and a
premarket-evaluation concept built on "competency assessment" (non-clinical
benchmarking + clinical confirmation). Public comment is open through
**2026-10-19**.

## Why flagged

MedGemma's severity narration is exactly the generative-AI-in-a-device-
function pattern this paper scopes. It's the clearest signal yet that FDA is
building a GenAI-specific risk lens distinct from the existing SaMD/CDS
carve-outs CardiTrack currently relies on (the General Wellness policy and
the HCP-facing-only Clinical Decision Support Software carve-out, both
already on file). The comment window is still open — a live opportunity to
shape, or at least anticipate, how FDA will eventually treat narrative
severity output from a model like MedGemma.

## Next question

Does the discussion paper's risk framework distinguish "narration/explanation
of existing clinician-reviewed data" from "generative diagnostic/treatment
suggestions," and if so, which side does an unprompted severity-escalation
narrative (CardiTrack's core AI feature) fall on? Worth deciding whether
CardiTrack should file a comment before Oct 19.
