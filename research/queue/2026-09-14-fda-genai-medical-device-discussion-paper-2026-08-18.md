# FDA discussion paper proposes two-axis risk framework for generative-AI medical devices, comments due 19 Oct 2026

## Summary
FDA's Center for Devices and Radiological Health (via the Digital Health Center of Excellence) released *Considerations for the Regulation of Generative AI-Enabled Medical Devices* on 18 August 2026 (docket FDA-2026-N-7874), a discussion paper and request for feedback — explicitly **not** draft or final guidance, and it takes no position on whether the discussed approaches fit within FDA's current legal authority. It proposes a two-axis risk-assessment framework for GenAI/LLM-enabled device software functions: (1) degree/independence of device action, ranging from non-directive informational output, through action-directing information, to supervised and then fully autonomous action-taking; (2) the severity of consequence if a user relies on an incorrect output, from limited to severe. Named risks include confabulations/hallucinations that can appear authentic, uncertainty in the bounds of a device's intended use, and performance drift between test and real-world environments; it separately flags agentic AI as posing oversight and post-market surveillance challenges existing mechanisms weren't built for. FDA also signals plans to tag devices using LLMs/foundation models on its public AI-Enabled Medical Device List and asks sponsors to proactively disclose LLM-based functionality in public summaries. The paper contains 26 discussion questions; partial responses on any subset are explicitly invited. Comments are due via Regulations.gov by 19 October 2026.

## Why flagged (tie to CardiTrack specifically)
This is the first FDA articulation of a concrete risk-scoring framework specifically for LLM-narration-style functions rather than classic ML/SaMD, and it lands squarely on CardiTrack's own design choice — MedGemma narrates but does not autonomously diagnose. Two things matter for us directly:
1. **Where we sit on the proposed autonomy axis.** CardiTrack's narration output is "action-directing information" at most (it may prompt a caregiver to call a clinician) and never "autonomous action" — that's the safest zone on FDA's own axis, and this paper is the first primary-source articulation we can cite that this positioning is meaningfully different from an autonomous/diagnostic system. Worth preserving verbatim in our own regulatory positioning file.
2. **Disclosure expectations are moving toward mandatory-in-practice.** FDA "encouraging" LLM-functionality disclosure in public summaries and device-list tagging today reads like the soft opening move that preceded the CDS/General Wellness carve-outs finalized in January 2026 — i.e., today's voluntary ask often becomes tomorrow's expectation. CardiTrack should be ready to disclose MedGemma's foundation-model nature in any public-facing materials before FDA formalizes this.
3. **Hallucination as a named regulatory risk category** is directly relevant to our SSA pre-processing/severity-routing design (`docs/llm_design.md`) — it strengthens the case for keeping the human-reviewed, non-autonomous-action design as the core regulatory defense, since this is exactly the axis FDA proposes to score consequence-of-error against.
This is US/second-priority per our beat ordering, and it's FYI-only since it's a comment request, not policy — but the comment window being open now is itself actionable: CardiTrack (or its counsel) could submit input framing narration-only, human-reviewed LLM outputs as the low end of both axes, potentially shaping how the eventual guidance treats products like ours.
