# UK National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare publishes final recommendations

**Severity:** HIGH
**Category:** regulation

## Summary

An MHRA-convened independent commission — chaired by NHS doctors, drawing on evidence from
12,000+ respondents over a year — published its final report on 2026-09-10, recommending a
future UK regulatory framework for AI in healthcare: continuous real-world post-market
monitoring for AI-enabled medical devices (not just point-of-approval clearance), oversight
intensity proportionate to patient risk rather than a fixed device/non-device binary, explicit
contractual allocation of risk/responsibility between device manufacturers and healthcare
providers, stronger MHRA enforcement powers, a public-facing searchable safety/incident database
for named AI devices, and a "proportionate" expectation that patients should be able to know
when AI is used in their care.

This is recommendations, not law — no dated compliance obligation yet — so it does not change
CardiTrack's SaMD status today. But two threads are worth tracking closely: the
risk-proportionate framing is exactly the kind of test that could eventually decide whether a
statistical-anomaly-narration tool (SSA signal → MedGemma plain-language narration, family
caregiver-facing, no clinical claims) sits inside or outside SaMD scope; and the
patient/caregiver-transparency recommendation is consumer-facing language CardiTrack should be
able to satisfy already, but is worth confirming against current app-store copy and in-app
disclosure of MedGemma's role.

## Why flagged

A credible, MHRA-convened commission's recommendations are the earliest visible signal of where
UK AI-in-healthcare regulation is heading — before any of it becomes a dated instrument. Given
CardiTrack's UK-first jurisdiction priority and the standing question of its SaMD boundary, this
is worth tracking now rather than waiting for a consultation.
