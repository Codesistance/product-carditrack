# MHRA-commissioned National AI-in-Healthcare Commission publishes final recommendations

**Severity:** HIGH
**Category:** regulation
**Date found:** 2026-09-12 (published 2026-09-10)

## Summary

The National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare (established
~September 2025, MHRA-commissioned, led by NHS doctors) published its final
report on 2026-09-10 after a public consultation with 12,000+ clinicians and
patients. Headline recommendations:

1. Staged, "L-plate"-style authorisation for new AI models — deployed under
   close supervision before fuller authorisation.
2. Continuous, real-world lifecycle monitoring rather than one-off approval,
   with clearer change-management procedures for updates to a deployed model.
3. A core principle that AI must augment, never replace, human clinical
   judgement, with meaningful human oversight and transparency about when and
   how AI is used in care.

This is recommendations only — MHRA/government will now consider them before
any formal regulatory response or legislative change. It's the clearest
signal yet of where the UK's dedicated AI-medical-device framework (flagged
since MHRA AI Airlock Phase 2, already tracked 2026-09-04) is heading.

The human-oversight / no-automated-decision principle is directionally
favourable to CardiTrack's narration-only MedGemma design (MedGemma explains,
never decides). But "staged authorisation" and continuous lifecycle
monitoring could add a new compliance layer even for narration-tier tools,
depending on how MHRA scopes "AI used in care" in its eventual formal
response.

## Why flagged

Moves the standing "where's the SaMD boundary" question — this is the
commission whose output MHRA is expected to act on for the framework flagged
in the AI Airlock Phase 2 report.

## Next question

Track MHRA's formal response (not yet issued) — does staged authorisation
apply to explanation/narration AI, or only to diagnostic/decision-support AI?
That answer determines whether CardiTrack's MedGemma layer falls inside or
outside the future authorisation regime.
