# MHRA National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare — final recommendations published

**Severity:** HIGH
**Category:** regulation
**Date flagged:** 2026-09-17

## Summary

The independent Commission (established by MHRA in September 2025, chaired
by Prof. Alastair Denniston) published its final report on 2026-09-10 after
a year-long evidence process involving 12,000+ contributors. Key
recommendations:

1. A staged "learner-phase" authorisation for new AI models — deployed
   under tight guardrails to prove real-world safety before full approval.
2. Continuous post-market / lifecycle monitoring rather than point-in-time
   evaluation.
3. A public, searchable registry of AI-enabled medical device safety and
   incident data.
4. A stated intent to reclassify many AI-based health products into higher
   risk categories under UK law.

This is a recommendations report, not yet binding regulation — it is
intended to feed the MHRA's future regulatory framework and the
already-reported Health Bill reform amendments.

## Why flagged

This is the first concrete articulation of the "reclassify AI health
products to a higher risk tier" direction that the standing SaMD-boundary
question has been watching for. If the eventual framework treats
severity-routing/digest outputs generated from AI analysis of cardiac
wearable data as needing "learner-phase" authorisation — regardless of the
current "narrates, does not diagnose" design posture — CardiTrack's AI
pipeline could be pulled toward SaMD-equivalent obligations sooner than the
EU MDCG borderline manual (already tracked) currently implies.

Not yet binding, so not CRITICAL — but the direction of travel and the
volume of process behind it (a year-long national Commission) make this a
signal worth active tracking rather than filing as routine FYI.
