# MHRA's National Commission on AI in Healthcare publishes recommendations — names direct-to-consumer wearables/apps for tailored guidance

**Severity:** HIGH
**Category:** regulation

## Summary

The National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare — established by the
MHRA in September 2025, NHS-doctor-led, ~12,000 stakeholders consulted — published its
final recommendations on **2026-09-10**. Headline asks relevant to CardiTrack:

- MHRA should issue **specific guidance for direct-to-consumer (DTC) applications and
  wearables**, using a "proportionate approach" that balances empowering people with
  health information against DTC-specific risks — rather than folding DTC products into
  existing SaMD/wellness guidance built for other contexts.
- **Stronger cybersecurity requirements** for consumer health apps and wearables.
- Staged/graduated authorisation for new AI models (real-world deployment under close
  supervision before full authorisation).
- Continuous real-world post-market monitoring of AI-enabled devices, not just
  point-in-time approval.
- A public-searchable safety/incident database for AI-enabled devices.
- Enhanced MHRA enforcement powers, and explicit contractual risk-allocation between
  AI device makers and healthcare providers.

MHRA has indicated it intends to begin operationalising a response "by September 2026,"
with legislative implementation in 2027 — this Commission report is the direct input
to the "AI-in-medical-devices framework expected later in 2026" already on the log from
the AI Airlock Phase 2 item.

## Why flagged

This is the first UK regulatory document to explicitly single out **direct-to-consumer
wearables/apps** as needing their own tailored guidance rather than being assessed
purely under existing SaMD/wellness rules — which is exactly CardiTrack's category
(caregiver-facing, not HCP-facing, built entirely on Google Health API wearable data).
It doesn't move the wellness/SaMD boundary today, but it signals MHRA is about to draw
a DTC-specific line, and "staged authorisation" + "continuous real-world monitoring" +
mandatory incident-searchability, if adopted for consumer AI, would be new obligations
beyond current MDR/UKCA practice — obligations an MHRA-only company would face before
a comparable device in most other jurisdictions.
