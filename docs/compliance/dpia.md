### 1.1 Scope and method

The repository contains two layers that materially disagree:

- **Part A — current state**: what is implemented in `src/` and deployed by `infrastructure/*.tf` (GCP `europe-west2`, Cloud Run, Cloud SQL PostgreSQL, 10-minute polling ingestion **plus the webhook/Pub/Sub pipeline running in dev**, Gemini and self-hosted MedGemma inference, **and push delivery via FCM HTTP v1** — the pipeline and push moved here from Part B in v0.6, rows A11–A13 and A18).
- **Part B — planned state**: what the design documents describe but which is not built (email and SMS notification channels, Stripe billing, consent records, retention workers beyond the partition drops). GDPR requires a DPIA **before** high-risk processing begins, so Part B is assessed prospectively; the processing in Part B must not launch until its risks are mitigated.

Contradictions between the two layers are catalogued in Appendix A. Where a documented control does not exist in code, this DPIA treats it as **absent**, not as present.

### 1.2 Current processing status

Real health data from up to 100 enrolled test users is (or is about to be) processed under Google's unverified-app cap ([docs/technical/oauth_clients.md](../technical/oauth_clients.md)). The system is **not** in general production. Consequences:

- Gaps in Part A are **active exposures for test users**, not hypothetical: remediation items marked **P0** in §10 should be completed (or test-user processing paused/scoped) as a priority.
- Items marked **launch blocker** must be complete before exceeding the test population or passing Google restricted-scope verification / CASA assessment.

## 2. Threshold assessment — is a DPIA required?

Assessed against the EDPB (WP248) nine criteria; two or more generally makes a DPIA mandatory. CardiTrack meets **seven**:

|---|---|---|---|

Planned: qualitative trend interpretation (the per-user LSTM risk scores were dropped 2026-08-10 — `docs/llm_design.md`). |
| 2 | Automated decision-making with significant effect | **Yes (implemented, dev)** | LLM severity classification decides whether family are alerted in real time — a decision that dispatches a human to check on the wearer; running in dev (A13/A18; no SMS exists). | 4 | Sensitive / special-category data | **Yes** | Health data throughout (Art. 9): heart rate **and its overnight variability**, SpO2, sleep architecture, **respiratory rate while asleep**, **cardiac effort (time in heart-rate zones)**, **unbroken inactivity**, medical notes (§4.2). |
| 5 | Large scale | **Prospectively** | Target market framing in `docs/solution_manifest.md`; currently ≤100 test users. |

**Conclusion: a DPIA is mandatory under EU and UK GDPR.** Washington MHMD and several US state laws additionally require consumer-health-data privacy policies and, in some states (e.g. Colorado, Connecticut), data-protection assessments for profiling/sensitive data — this document is structured to serve those too.

### 2.1 Regulatory-boundary flag: early-warning positioning

CardiTrack is positioned as an **early-warning system based on trends and patterns**, not a diagnostic tool; the live MedGemma tone block states "Never diagnose or invent a condition" (`MedicalPromptBlocks.Tone`, `docs/llm_design.md`). However, software intended to **predict or prognose** disease can qualify as a medical device under EU MDR (Rule 11) and UK MDR even without diagnosing, and FDA has analogous software-as-medical-device criteria. **`[DECISION REQUIRED — OI-2]`**: a formal device-classification assessment must be completed before launch marketing settles on claims. This DPIA does not assume either outcome. The EU AI Act's Annex I (AI in a regulated product) route depends on the same answer — see [ai_act_classification.md](ai_act_classification.md) §5, and OI-15 for the separate Annex III question.

**MedGemma's region, and why it is a region change and not a transfer.** From 2026-08-21 clinical inference runs on one shared L4 Cloud Run service, `carditrack-common-medgemma`, in **`europe-west1` (Belgium)** — Cloud Run offers no L4 accelerator in `europe-west2`, which is why it moved at all. The per-environment CPU service in `europe-west2` is destroyed.

Three things this does not change, stated because a region line in a hosting row invites the opposite assumption. The **processor is the same** — Google Cloud, under the same Cloud DPA, not a new recipient. The **transfer position is unchanged**: both regions are inside the EU/EEA, so no new transfer mechanism arises and OI-5 (which is about US-linked transfers) is untouched. And the **data is identical**: A5's member context block — age, sex, free-text `MedicalNotes` truncated at 1000 chars, consent-gated exercise conditions, three questionnaire answers — reaches MedGemma exactly as before, and reaches nothing else.

What *did* change is concentration. One instance now serves every environment, where each previously had its own. Nothing about an individual record's exposure changes, but the blast radius of a misconfiguration does: a public IAM grant on this one service would expose the model behind dev and prod together. That is the risk PR #238's detection exists for, and the alert moved with the service ([`infrastructure/common/alerting.tf`](../../infrastructure/common/alerting.tf)) rather than being left behind with the thing it used to watch. It remains detective, not preventive — the org policy that would make such a grant impossible cannot exist in a project with no organization.

One further change worth stating plainly rather than leaving in Terraform. The per-environment service was `INGRESS_TRAFFIC_INTERNAL_ONLY`, which worked because the API's VPC egress carried its calls onto the internal path. That mechanism does not survive a cross-region move — the callers sit in `europe-west2` with `PRIVATE_RANGES_ONLY` egress and the service has no VPC attachment — so the shared service is `INGRESS_TRAFFIC_ALL` and **its endpoint is reachable from the internet**. Authorisation is now IAM alone: two named invoker service accounts, no `allUsers` binding, and the alert above. Unauthenticated requests do arrive and are refused (ten within twenty seconds on the day it went live), which is what a public endpoint receives. Nothing about who may actually invoke it changed; what was removed is the second, network-position layer that used to sit in front of the same IAM check.

**In-process libraries (not processors):** Math.NET Numerics (`MathNet.Numerics` 5.0.0, MIT) computes SSA eigen-decomposition and optional descriptive statistics inside the Worker / PipelineJobs process. No reading is transmitted. Inventory: [mathnet_numerics.md](../technical/mathnet_numerics.md). Do not add it to the processor table.

The hard problem: **the data subject may not be the consenting party.** The default flow has the caregiver recording the wearer's consent, possibly `verbal_confirmed`, with no wearer account, no notice to the wearer, and no withdrawal channel. Until resolved, this is the DPIA's largest open legal risk.

### 6.2 Minimisation and proportionality findings

- Ingestion is limited to three restricted read-only scopes. It is **no longer daily-only at rest**: the Worker cadence persists five minute-grain series — heart rate, steps, active-zone minutes, SpO2 and (from 2026-08-22) heart rate variability — into `GranularMetricHours` under a 90-day partition-drop retention, because the SSA/real-time assessment path in [llm_design.md](../llm_design.md) is a sub-daily analysis and cannot be run on daily rollups. Two reads remain intraday **in transit only**, reduced in memory with no sub-daily point persisted: SpO2's *daily* average/min/max (the API publishes no minimum or maximum in the daily summary) and the `activity-level` interval series, which is read solely to compute one number per day — the longest unbroken sedentary stretch — and discarded. The proportionality claim for the stored series is therefore narrower than "daily granularity": it is that each stored series feeds a named detection path, retention is 90 days by partition drop, and no sub-daily point leaves the estate.
- Fields collected but unused (`StressScore` — unfillable from this API at all, `PasswordHash`, `PaymentMethod`) should be removed or justified.
- Precise date of birth is required; consider whether year alone would serve the age-gate and baseline purposes.
- Gemini receives the member's **name** in report prompts (A6) — a positional "Patient A" label now replaces it before the call (finding 7 closed). Chat (A4) used to send the member GUID and **no longer does**. The MedGemma insight prompts (A5) apply the opposite rule deliberately: age and sex go in because they change how a heart rate or sleep duration should be read, while name and member id are excluded because they change nothing about the clinical reading.
- `MedicalNotes` in A5 is the most sensitive field the system holds and it is now sent verbatim (truncated) to the model. It earns its place — conditions and medication are invisible to a wearable and change the interpretation — and it is stored **encrypted at rest** (AES-256-GCM, `CardiMemberService`; M1 closed). The remaining condition: the note is uncontrolled free text (R-A14).
- Push payload design (B3) puts health state on lock screens by default — offer a discreet-notification option.
- `CanViewHealthData` defaults to **true** for every new relationship — default-on sharing of Art. 9 data should be an explicit choice.
